Multi-Rate Key Findings

Decoupling fast optogenetic-driven responses from slow continuous baseline integration enables high-frequency transients capture. Increasing macro-to-micro sub-stepping factor from M=1 to M=10 consistently improves test R² from 0.9111 to 0.9130 when using the zero_fast initialization strategy. Learnable splitting parameterization appears prone to minor validation overfitting, making the direct zero-fast split the recommended protocol.

Skip Connections Key Findings

Simpler skip connections resolve the massive overfitting seen in the raw MLP skips (which dropped R² to 0.8895). Applying a heavy separate weight decay (swd=1.0) on the linear skip connection layers achieves a test R² of 0.9144, but still fails to outperform the pure continuous-time CA-NODE baseline ($R^2 \approx 0.9219$). This indicates that adding direct feedforward paths bypassed ODE regularizing properties without offering substantial predictive advantage.

Causal Latent Key Findings

Enforcing strict causality (where the GRU encoder only sees the past window to infer initial state $z_0$ and forecasts forward) degrades prediction accuracy to $R^2 \approx 0.4405$ compared to non-causal smoothing ($R^2 \approx 0.90$). However, increasing historical context window size from 50ms to 200ms shows a clear monotonic improvement in $R^2$, indicating that longer temporal contexts are crucial for continuous latent state tracking in electrophysiology systems.
